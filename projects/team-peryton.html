01 / THE MISSION

More than an airframe: a complete autonomous aircraft system.

The IMechE UAS Challenge required teams to design and demonstrate a fixed-wing unmanned aircraft capable of autonomous flight, waypoint navigation and payload operations within a defined safety area.

The project joined aerodynamics, structures, propulsion, avionics, software and field operations into one flight-ready platform.

02 / SYSTEM INTEGRATION

Every subsystem had to work as one aircraft.

The avionics architecture connected navigation, air-data, telemetry, propulsion and five servos through a Matek flight controller, with separate high-current propulsion and regulated avionics power paths.

03 / FLIGHT SYSTEM

A flying wing built around payload, efficiency and stable autonomous operation.

ConfigurationFlying-wing layout using a reflexed aerofoil, moderate sweep, washout and wingtip stability features.
PropulsionA single-propeller system was selected to reduce mass, cost and integration complexity.
Mission tradePayload, waypoint completion, water carriage, efficiency and handling quality drove the design choices.

04 / MISSION & FLIGHT TEST

Plan the route. Fly the aircraft. Read the difference.

The mission plan defined the intended waypoint sequence and safety boundary. Post-flight replay then exposed the actual path, mode changes and repeated manoeuvres needed during development.

05 / ENGINEERING TRADE-OFFS

Decisions were scored against the mission—not personal preference.

Weighted selection matrices gave manufacturing effort and aircraft mass greater influence while retaining aerodynamic, slosh and structural considerations.

06 / SUB-CASE STUDY

When the aircraft came down, the log became the evidence.

A loss-of-control event initially appeared to indicate a control-surface problem. Comparing desired and measured attitude, RC inputs, angle of attack, speed, altitude and elevon outputs produced a more defensible explanation.

The data showed a steep climb followed by a recorded full-left roll demand, high estimated angle of attack, output saturation and insufficient altitude for recovery. It did not, on its own, prove a mechanical surface failure.

07 / LESSONS

Flight testing is an evidence loop.

The value of the programme was not only the competition result, but the practical discipline of preparing, observing and learning from real aircraft behaviour.

  • Configure conservatively. Initial attitude limits should match the tested envelope and available recovery height.
  • Compare commands with response. Desired-versus-measured plots separate controller demand from aircraft behaviour.
  • Preserve the evidence. RC, actuator, air-data and event logging turn assumptions into testable findings.
  • Verify the physical system. Logged servo output is not the same as measured control-surface position.
